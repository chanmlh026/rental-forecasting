## 3. Record Approved Methodology and Rationale

Update the Data Ingestion Development Journal with the following owner decisions and rationale.

### Decision 1 — Annual Earnings Rather Than Hourly Earnings

**Owner decision:** Use gross annual earnings.

**Owner rationale:**

The purpose of this predictor is to represent the earning capacity of people living in a Local Authority, particularly their potential ability to afford rent.

Annual earnings reflect both pay rates and the amount of employment undertaken.

An employee with high hourly pay but relatively few working hours may have limited total earnings and therefore limited capacity to afford higher rent.

This is why the owner considers annual earnings more appropriate than hourly earnings for this predictor.

### Decision 2 — Median Rather Than Mean

**Owner decision:** Use median earnings.

**Owner rationale:**

The distribution of earnings may be skewed by high earners.

The median is preferred because it is less sensitive to extreme high values and is intended to provide a more representative measure of typical employee earnings.

### Decision 3 — Include All Employees

**Owner decision:** Include full-time and part-time employees, regardless of sex.

**Owner rationale:**

The objective is to understand the overall earnings capacity of local employees.

Part-time employment is deliberately included because fewer hours worked may reduce total annual earnings and potential rental affordability, even when hourly pay is relatively high.

Separate full-time/part-time or male/female predictors are not required for the approved initial measure.

### Decision 4 — Acknowledge the Household Income Limitation

**Owner-acknowledged limitation:**

Individual employee earnings are not equivalent to household income or household rental affordability.

For example, a household with two earners may have greater total resources than a household with one earner, even if its individual median earnings are lower.

The owner accepts this limitation at the data-ingestion stage.

During later model development, the project will assess:

- Whether median annual employee earnings contribute useful predictive information.
- Whether the measure should be retained as a model feature.
- How its relationship with rental prices should be interpreted.
- Whether the difference between individual earnings and household income materially affects interpretation.

Do not treat this limitation as a reason to reject the dataset during ingestion.

Do not claim that the variable has already demonstrated predictive usefulness.
