# 004.2. Finalise Forecast Origin, Target Horizon and Publication Cutoff Definitions

Please update data-architecture-and-dictionary.md to reflect the following confirmed project decision.

## Confirmed Decision: Option A

The rental forecasting project will use a **12-calendar-month forecasting horizon measured from the latest available published rent observation month**, rather than from the calendar month in which the forecast is issued.

### 1. Define the forecast origin

- forecast\_issue\_date: The actual date the forecast is generated.

- forecast\_cutoff\_at: The precise information cutoff timestamp, including timezone.

- origin\_month: The latest eligible published monthly rent observation available at the forecast cutoff.

- target\_month: Exactly 12 calendar months after origin\_month.

- forecast\_horizon\_months: 12.

Example (illustrative, subject to source release verification):

- Forecast issued: 2026-10-22

- Latest eligible rent observation: 2026-09

- Forecast origin: 2026-09

- Target month: 2027-09

### 2. Define the target

For local authority LA, bedroom category b, and origin month t:

y(LA, b, t) = Rent(LA, b, t + 12 months)

The target is the future average monthly private rent in GBP, not rental growth.

### 3. Define feature availability

A feature may be used only if its publication or availability timestamp is no later than forecast\_cutoff\_at.

The feature's observation period may be earlier than, equal to, or later than the rent origin month, provided that the information was genuinely available by the forecast cutoff.

Preserve observation periods, release timestamps, source vintages, and retrieval metadata.

### 4. Define historical backtesting

Each historical forecast must reconstruct:

- Its historical forecast issue/cutoff timestamp.

- The latest rent observation genuinely available at that cutoff.

- The corresponding 12-month-ahead target month.

- The features and data vintages available at that cutoff.

- The target labels available by the model-training cutoff.

Clearly distinguish exact point-in-time reconstruction from approximate retrospective backtesting when archived source vintages are unavailable.

### 5. Address data coverage and consistency

Investigate whether the latest eligible rent month can differ across local authorities or bedroom categories.

Recommend a consistent origin-month policy for cross-area comparisons, and document how missing or delayed series will be handled without target imputation.

Do not assume that all requested LA-by-bedroom series exist until verified against the official ONS PIPR tables.
