# 006.2. Predictor Data Availability and Quality Exploration

## Objective

Conduct an evidence-based investigation into the availability, historical coverage, geographic granularity, accessibility, and quality of candidate predictor datasets for the UK Rental Forecasting Project.

This is a **research and exploratory data assessment task**, not a production data ingestion or feature engineering task.

The objectives are to:

1. Establish which candidate predictor datasets are available and suitable for subsequent ingestion investigation.
2. Identify their data characteristics, limitations, and unresolved questions.
3. Provide evidence for informed project-owner decisions.
4. Recommend a practical sequence of production ingestion batches.

Do not establish arbitrary data acceptance thresholds, exclude candidate predictors based on quality concerns, or evaluate their statistical predictive usefulness.

- Review `docs/data-architecture-and-dictionary.md`.

The approved forecasting objective is to predict average monthly private rent 12 months ahead for 294 English Local Authorities, separately for 1-bedroom, 2-bedroom, and 3-bedroom properties.

Distinguish approved project decisions from proposals, assumptions, and historical documentation.

These standardised definitions are approved for investigation, not yet for implementation.

## 3. Investigation Requirements

### Step 1 — Review Existing Evidence

Identify which datasets and variables have already been investigated.

Classify their current verification status:

- Verified dataset and variable.
- Official source identified but partially verified.
- Candidate source requiring further investigation.
- Derived predictor dependent on underlying source data.

Reuse existing evidence where reliable.

Do not repeat the completed PIPR coverage audit unless there is a specific unresolved issue.

### Step 2 — Verify Dataset Availability

For each underlying source dataset, investigate:

1. Official publisher and exact dataset name.
2. Official URL, series identifier, or table reference.
3. Variable definition and measurement units.
4. Geographic granularity, particularly Local Authority availability.
5. Historical start and end dates.
6. Observation frequency.
7. Availability across the project's 294 Local Authorities, where applicable.
8. Data access method, available formats, automation feasibility, and licensing considerations.

Do not assume a general dataset landing page establishes the availability of a particular variable.

Where necessary and practical, inspect actual official files, API responses, or representative extracts to verify metadata and coverage.

### Step 3 — Explore Data Quality and Limitations

Investigate the following characteristics where evidence is available:

- Missing observations and geographic coverage gaps.
- Duplicate or inconsistent records.
- Invalid or unusual values.
- Historical definition changes.
- Local Authority boundary changes.
- Statistical estimation uncertainty and sampling limitations.
- Suppressed or unavailable observations.
- Publication frequency and typical publication delays.
- Historical revisions and archived data-vintage availability.
- Earliest feasible historical observation.
- Earliest defensible point-in-time availability.
- Limitations relevant to subsequent ingestion, preprocessing, or modelling.

Clearly distinguish:

- Observation dates.
- Publication or availability dates.
- Data retrieval dates.
- Source release vintages.

Record relevant limitations without automatically treating them as reasons for excluding predictors.

**Do not create pass/fail thresholds, arbitrary quality scores, or final dataset acceptance decisions.**

### Step 4 — Apply Proportionate Exploration

The objective is to establish reliable evidence for subsequent ingestion decisions, not to perform exhaustive production-level data validation.

Prioritise:

1. Confirming the correct official source and variable definition.
2. Establishing historical and geographic coverage.
3. Understanding source frequency and publication timing.
4. Identifying material data-quality and consistency limitations.
5. Confirming that the data can be accessed programmatically where feasible.

Use targeted extracts and representative samples where these provide sufficient evidence.

Perform wider quantitative coverage analysis when necessary to establish important dataset characteristics.

Avoid unnecessarily downloading and processing complete historical datasets when equivalent reliable evidence already exists.

Reserve comprehensive validation, production parsing, recovery testing, and complete data-quality checks for the subsequent ingestion tasks.

Do not invent coverage statistics or treat sampled observations as proof of complete dataset coverage.

### Step 5 — Recommend Production Ingestion Batches

After investigating the candidate datasets, propose a practical implementation roadmap for the remaining Data Ingestion phase.

Group related datasets where appropriate, considering:

- Common official publishers.
- Similar data access methods and formats.
- Shared ingestion requirements.
- Observation frequencies and geographic granularity.
- Historical coverage and publication characteristics.
- Dependencies between underlying datasets.
- Implementation complexity and validation requirements.

The roadmap should balance efficiency with independent verification.

Avoid both extremes:

- One large task implementing every data source without intermediate review.
- Excessively fragmented tasks where closely related sources could reasonably be implemented together.

For each proposed batch, provide:

1. Proposed task number and title.
2. Datasets included.
3. Rationale for grouping.
4. Important implementation considerations.
5. Dependencies or unresolved methodological decisions.
6. Suggested validation checkpoint.

Retain Task 007 for Bank of England Bank Rate and IUMZID4 ingestion unless evidence justifies recommending a change.

Do not implement any production ingestion tasks during this investigation.

The project owner will review and approve the roadmap before implementation.

## 5. Explicit Exclusions

Do not undertake:

- Predictor correlation analysis.
- Target-predictor correlation analysis.
- Multicollinearity analysis or treatment.
- Predictive usefulness evaluation.
- Feature selection or backward elimination.
- Feature scaling, encoding, or imputation.
- ML training, tuning, or backtesting.
- Production data ingestion implementation.
- Construction of a model-ready feature matrix.
- New GCP infrastructure or deployment work.
- Unauthorised changes to approved methodology or architecture.

Do not automatically reject datasets because of missing values, publication delays, limited geographic coverage, historical revisions, or statistical uncertainty.

These characteristics should be investigated and documented.

Their implications for preprocessing, modelling, or final feature selection will be considered in subsequent project phases.

## 6. Evidence and Research Principles

Follow these principles:

- Prefer authoritative primary sources, particularly official UK statistical publishers.
- Distinguish verified facts from assumptions and unverified claims.
- Do not invent historical coverage, publication dates, or completeness statistics.
- Inspect actual datasets where necessary and feasible.
- Prefer targeted investigation over unnecessary large downloads.
- Retain relevant source references, inspection dates, and reproducible evidence.
- Preserve the distinction between raw measurements and proposed derived features.
- Do not silently substitute alternative variables or geographic definitions.
- Record unresolved limitations transparently.
- Prefer clear, maintainable documentation over unnecessary complexity.

Routine research and technical investigation decisions may be handled independently.

Seek project-owner approval before material methodological changes.

## 8. Acceptance Criteria

The investigation is complete when:

1. Every candidate predictor has been assessed or explicitly recorded as unresolved.
2. Every reported source and historical coverage claim has identifiable supporting evidence.
3. Geographic availability and historical completeness are documented where verifiable.
4. Publication delays, revisions, and statistical limitations are documented without imposing arbitrary acceptance thresholds.
5. Derived predictors are linked to their underlying source datasets and relevant construction requirements.
6. Housing-supply standardisation requirements and unresolved issues are documented.
7. A logical and justified production ingestion roadmap has been proposed.
8. Existing approved project decisions remain unchanged.
9. No production ingestion, feature engineering, preprocessing, or modelling functionality has been implemented.
10. Unverified findings and outstanding decisions are explicitly reported.

Completion does not require every candidate dataset to be successfully verified or approved for modelling.

It requires the investigation to be conducted transparently, with evidence sufficient to support subsequent development decisions where feasible.
