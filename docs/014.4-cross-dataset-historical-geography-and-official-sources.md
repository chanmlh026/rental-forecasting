# 014.4 – Cross-Dataset Historical Geography and Official Source Investigation

## Objective

Investigate whether official historical datasets provide geographically comparable observations for the 11 reorganised English Local Authorities identified in Tasks 014.2 and 014.3.

Prioritise existing official data over reconstruction, approximation or imputation.

This is a **research-only task**. No preprocessing implementation or methodology decision is authorised.

## Scope

Investigate all relevant LA-level variables currently included in the Rental Forecasting Project:

1. ASHE median gross annual earnings
2. Model-based unemployment rate (UMB)
3. Population
4. Population density
5. Migration
6. Dwelling stock
7. Housing supply, including completions and planning statistics
8. UK House Price Index and average house prices
9. ONS PIPR rental prices, to verify target geography consistency

Use the exact 11 reorganised authorities documented in Task 014.3. Do not expand the cohort without identifying a material additional geography issue.

Exclude national-level predictors without LA boundaries.

## Investigation Requirements

### 1. Review Existing Evidence

Review relevant source ingestions, data dictionaries, audit reports, Issues Register and Tasks 014.1–014.3.

Check the latest `main` and existing research branches before starting. Preserve any unmerged work.

### 2. Identify Official Historical Sources

Investigate whether ONS, Nomis, MHCLG, HM Land Registry or other existing official sources provide retrospective data on boundaries compatible with our April 2025 reference geography.

Prioritise:

- Existing ingested datasets and retained source files.
- Official metadata, methodology documents and geography notes.
- Published historical extracts or backcast datasets.
- Alternative geographic versions of existing official datasets.

Do not substitute different statistical definitions without explicitly flagging them for owner consideration.

### 3. Verify Geographic Comparability

For each variable, authority and relevant historical year, distinguish:

- **Confirmed:** Official evidence supports compatible geographic boundaries.
- **Unverified:** Numeric observations exist, but geographic comparability is not established.
- **Predecessor-only:** Data exist for former authorities, but not for the required combined authority.
- **Missing:** No suitable observation identified in the sources investigated.
- **Evidence gap:** Source availability or geographic definition cannot yet be established.

Do not assume matching current GSS codes prove historical geographic equivalence.

Distinguish observation year, geographic reference date and publication vintage. Record whether retrospective data would have been available at the relevant historical forecast date; do not assume later backcasts were available earlier.

### 4. Produce a Cross-Dataset Coverage Assessment

Provide a clear summary table showing the status of each variable across all 11 authorities.

Retain detailed authority × variable × year evidence, including source references, original geography, coverage and unresolved limitations.

Identify:

- Which historical series can potentially be used without reconstruction.
- Which sources require further verification.
- Which gaps could potentially be addressed through aggregation of compatible counts.
- Which non-additive statistics require separate methodological decisions.
- Which gaps remain after the official-source investigation.

Do not calculate reconstructed values.

### 5. Recommend Next Methodological Decisions

Summarise the remaining issues in priority order.

Distinguish decisions requiring project-owner approval from technical implementation that Codex can handle later.

Do not approve any source substitution, geography mapping, approximation, imputation or modelling policy.
