The two known historical evidence versions are in commits
`f73119a05b6eea046c61cac47954f19e9797da48` and
`321962d0683a1e01953115e8b9f2e555cb8dd3b6`. Both are ancestors of remote main
and the existing published development branches. Two unauthenticated HEAD

No response bodies were downloaded by those HEAD checks; no Bank session was
replayed. This is verified public accessibility, not merely a hypothetical risk.
Holding the new earnings/UMB merges does not remove the already reachable history.

two retained Set-Cookie fields with two distinct identifiers, reused in both

Secure, host-only scope and root path, no explicit Expires/Max-Age, and no explicit
HttpOnly/SameSite attributes in the retained headers. Cookie values were neither
printed nor included in this report/evidence; only counts and attributes are
recorded in [local assessment evidence](../reports/data_audits/009.5/security-assessment-evidence.json).

The known identifiers do not appear in any currently tracked file on this
review tree or in the six inspected public remote branch-tip trees. This is a
known-value scan, not a claim that every repository object is free of all secrets.
The preventive allowlist remains implemented at capture/publication/export;

credential, authenticated Bank account or personal records is established by
these public statistical requests. Who may have downloaded the public history,
and any existing clones/caches/forks or misuse, remain unknown.

### Can they still matter?

**Potentially, but current validity and impact are unverified.** The
[Bank's cookie statement](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/legal/privacy-and-cookie-policy)
identifies ASPSESSIONID cookies as database browsing-session identifiers and
describes end-of-session expiry. That supports their public browsing origin;
it does not prove the particular server-side sessions are now invalid or harmless.

No explicit browser expiry was recorded. Client session lifetime and server-side
session validity are different questions. Microsoft's
[classic ASP configuration documentation](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/iis/configuration/system.webserver/asp/session)
documents a configurable inactivity timeout (default 20 minutes); there is no
evidence of the Bank's actual configuration or last activity for these sessions.
The default therefore cannot be used as proof of expiry. Transport/browser flags
are not a revocation mechanism for identifiers that have already been disclosed.
No replay, active validity probe, publisher contact or invalidation was performed.

If a disclosed identifier still resolves to meaningful server-side session state,
possession could expose or influence that browsing session. The contents, privilege
level and actual reach of that state are not known, so neither account compromise
nor harmlessness is asserted. Treat the identifiers as exposed and potentially
usable pending authoritative confirmation, rather than reporting them as expired.
