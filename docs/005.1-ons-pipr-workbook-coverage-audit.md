# 005.1. ONS PIPR Workbook Coverage Audit

**Audit type:** Data feasibility and quality review; no ingestion pipeline or
forecast model was implemented.

## A. Executive summary

The latest inspected ONS Price Index of Private Rents (PIPR) workbook contains
an actual monthly average-rent panel for 294 English Local Authorities (LAs),
each with separate 1-, 2-, and 3-bedroom rent levels. The 21 October 2026
edition contains complete positive numeric values from January 2015 through
September 2026 for all 882 eligible LA-bedroom series:

- Expected and valid target cells: **124,362 of 124,362 (100%)**.
- Missing, duplicate, or non-positive target cells: **0**.
- LA-bedroom series with any missing month: **0 of 882**.
- Structurally complete 12-month origin-target pairs: **113,778**.
- Latest common observation month in this edition: **September 2026**.

The literal project objective of a target for **every one of England's 296
LAs** is not supported by this workbook: ONS does not publish City of London
or Isles of Scilly in the inspected target table. These are reported as known
geographic exclusions, not as missing monthly observations. Therefore the
verdict is **CONDITIONALLY FEASIBLE**, provided the project explicitly accepts
the 294 published authorities as its target cohort. No rent target was
imputed.

The March 2024 archived workbook contains the same target table and
January 2015-February 2024 historical rent levels, but lacks the complete
LA coverage of the latest edition: Barnsley and Sheffield are absent. Many
overlapping values differ between those two vintages. Those differences are
verified, but their cause is not established by this two-workbook comparison.
The available evidence does not support strict point-in-time (PIT)
reconstruction for all historical forecast origins, particularly before
PIPR's first publication in March 2024.

## C. Target structure verification

The required LA × bedroom × month panel is directly present in the workbook;
it is not inferred by combining separate tables. The audited fields are
monthly average rent **levels**, in pounds sterling per month, for 1-, 2-, and
3-bedroom properties. The displayed rent values are rounded to whole pounds.
They are ONS statistical estimates of market rents, not a census of individual
tenancy contracts. Rent levels must not be confused with the separate index
or growth-rate measures in the wider PIPR workbook and publication.

The table supplies a monthly `Time period`, `Area code`, and `Area name`. For
the audited English LA cohort, each area-month row contains the three
bedroom-level rent columns. Each of the 294 eligible authorities has all 141
months in the latest edition, January 2015 through September 2026.

## H. Geographic coverage

The denominator is based on ONS's *Local Authority Districts (April 2025)
Names and Codes in the UK (V2)* reference (ArcGIS item
`5779a9578f0e48ccacef6af41546b56b`), which lists **296 English LAs**. Codes
and names in the PIPR workbook were compared with this dated reference:

- 294 English LA codes/names have target series in the latest workbook.
- City of London (`E09000001`) and Isles of Scilly (`E06000053`) do not have
  target series in that workbook.
- Both exclusions are retained as explicit known geographic exclusions;
  their cause is not inferred from absence alone.
- All 294 published LA codes and names match the selected reference.

The 2025 reference is a current comparison population, not proof that every
historical PIPR value has been reconstructed on one constant boundary vintage.
The inspected workbook does not provide an effective-dated boundary vintage
for every observation. Historical LA reorganisations and any ONS treatment of
them therefore remain a geography-continuity issue to investigate before
creating a long-run canonical geography mapping. Preserve the source code and
release vintage; do not merge or average rents across changing boundaries
without a defensible official correspondence and weighting method.

Reference: [ONS Local Authority Districts (April 2025) names and codes, V2](https://www.arcgis.com/home/item.html?id=5779a9578f0e48ccacef6af41546b56b).

## I. Common-origin feasibility

The latest workbook edition is dated 21 October 2026 and its latest rent
observation month is September 2026. For the approved Option A convention,
September 2026 is the latest common origin supported by this edition, provided
that the forecast's `forecast_cutoff_at` is no earlier than the actual public
availability of that edition.

Coverage is 100% of the 882-series eligible cohort at every month, including
September 2026. Consequently:

| Exploratory threshold | Latest month meeting threshold | Eligible LA-bedroom values | Eligible series excluded |
|---:|---|---:|---:|
| 90% | September 2026 | 882 / 882 | 0 |
| 95% | September 2026 | 882 / 882 | 0 |
| 99% | September 2026 | 882 / 882 | 0 |
| 100% | September 2026 | 882 / 882 | 0 |

These are exploratory thresholds, not an approved operating rule. Relative to
all 296 English LAs, coverage is 882 / 888 = 99.32%; the full 100% requirement
would not be met because of the six structurally unavailable LA-bedroom
series. Do not silently drop these authorities or substitute imputed targets.

Recommended policy: approve a fixed 294-LA cohort for this target, select the
latest common origin month meeting a predeclared completeness rule for that
cohort, and report the actual coverage each run. If a future edition has a
missing or delayed target at the selected month, retain the row as unavailable
and either move the common origin back under the declared rule or exclude that
row from the corresponding evaluation without imputing its target.

## J. Training and backtesting implications

For each LA, bedroom category `b ∈ {1, 2, 3}`, and origin month `t`, the
preserved target definition is:

```text
y(LA, b, t) = Rent(LA, b, t + 12 calendar months)
```

The target is future average monthly rent in GBP, not rental growth. The
forecast issue date, timezone-aware information cutoff (`forecast_cutoff_at`),
latest eligible published rent month (`origin_month`), and
`target_month = origin_month + 12 calendar months` remain distinct. This audit
does not change the existing Option A decision in
[the architecture and data dictionary](./data-architecture-and-dictionary.md).

With 141 observed months, there are 129 possible origins per LA-bedroom
series. All origin and target endpoints are present and positive in the latest
workbook, yielding **113,778 structural pairs** (37,926 per bedroom), with
origins January 2015 through September 2025 and target months January 2016
through September 2026.

This is a structural eligibility count using the current workbook vintage; it
is **not** proof that each label or feature was public at a historical model
training cutoff. ONS first published PIPR in March 2024, although the released
series contains retrospective observations from January 2015. Backtests
claiming forecasts made before PIPR's first release cannot treat those
backfilled PIPR observations as then-public labels or features.

Feature values must be selected using `available_at <= forecast_cutoff_at`,
with observation periods, release timestamps, source vintages, and retrieval
metadata retained. Training labels must separately be available by the
historical model-training cutoff. Exact PIT reconstruction requires archived
source vintages and release availability evidence. Where those are missing,
label the exercise **approximate retrospective backtesting**, state the
availability-lag and vintage assumptions, and do not present it as exact PIT.

Target missingness does not currently reduce structural pair counts in this
vintage. If future target values are missing, do not create training labels
for those endpoint pairs through interpolation, forward fill, or other
imputation. Missing lagged rent features may be handled only under documented
feature rules and PIT availability constraints. Rolling features must use
past-only windows. Cross-validation must be chronological, and cross-area
comparisons must use a consistent origin/target month and a documented
geographic cohort.

## K. Historical vintage findings

The March 2024 workbook contains the same PIPR target table, the LA and
bedroom-level rent columns, and historical observations from January 2015
through February 2024. It contains 292 of the 294 currently eligible LA
series. Barnsley (`E08000038`) and Sheffield (`E08000039`) are wholly absent
from the archived workbook; each absence represents 110 months × 3 bedroom
fields in that archive. The reason for their absence was not established.

For the 292 LAs with overlapping numeric values in both editions, there are
32,120 compared values per bedroom category (292 × 110):

| Bedroom | Numeric overlap | Values that differ | Share differing | Maximum absolute rent difference |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 32,120 | 26,809 | 83.47% | £36 |
| 2 | 32,120 | 26,310 | 81.91% | £61 |
| 3 | 32,120 | 27,007 | 84.08% | £78 |

The differences establish that the two observed vintages are not identical;
they do **not** establish whether changes arose from revisions, changed
methods, geography treatment, corrected data, or another cause. The
two-month revision policy described by ONS for the newest UK months does not,
by itself, explain these historic overlap differences. `vintage_comparison.csv`
contains the comparison counts and interpretation.

One March 2024 archive is not sufficient to reconstruct every historical
release. In particular, an archive edition cannot make PIPR information
available before its first publication. Preserve release-specific downloads
prospectively for future PIT work and treat older backtests as approximate
unless the necessary vintages and availability timestamps can be verified.

## L. Final feasibility verdict

**CONDITIONALLY FEASIBLE**

The exact monthly rent-level target is verified for **294 eligible English
LAs × 3 bedroom categories**, continuously from January 2015 to September
2026, with 100% completeness in the latest inspected workbook. It is **not**
verified for every English LA as originally worded: City of London and Isles
of Scilly are absent from the official target table. The verdict is
conditional on accepting the 294 published authorities and explicitly
excluding those two geographies from this target, rather than imputing or
silently dropping them.

The current edition supports a common September 2026 origin structurally.
Historical vintage differences and the absence of comprehensive historical
availability records limit strict PIT claims. These limitations do not
invalidate the published target levels, but must be addressed in ingestion,
release archiving, and backtest descriptions.

   of London and Isles of Scilly explicitly out of scope for this PIPR target.
2. Proceed with Task 006 target ingestion only under that approved scope:
   preserve original workbook releases, checksums, release dates, and

   bedroom fields, rent values, and coverage.
3. Record `published_at`/`available_at` for each acquired release and retain
   release vintages prospectively. Avoid claims of exact PIT reconstruction
   for historical periods lacking archived vintages.
4. Investigate the vintage differences, the two authorities absent from the
   March 2024 archive, and historical LA boundary continuity before defining
   a canonical long-run geography mapping.
5. Preserve the existing IUMZID4 mortgage-rate source and the confirmed
   Option A origin, 12-calendar-month horizon, and GBP rent-level target.
6. Review the ranked rent-movement flags and document any follow-up
   investigation; do not mark plausible large movements invalid without
   evidence.

**Task 006 readiness:** it can begin after approval of the 294-LA target
cohort. The workbook evidence is sufficient to design and validate the PIPR
ingestion for that scope. Historical boundary continuity, vintage causes,
and strict PIT reconstruction remain unresolved; they are blockers to making
strong retrospective PIT claims, not blockers to an explicitly vintage-aware
initial ingestion.
