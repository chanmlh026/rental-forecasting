### Verified measure and owner's decision

Owner approved **UMB first**, preferring model-based local unemployment because
small local APS samples can give unreliable direct estimates. APS is a fallback
only if UMB proves unsuitable; claimant count is not a substitute. The
[Nomis UMB source](https://www.nomisweb.co.uk/datasets/umb) supplies precisely the
selected published rate through dataset **NM_127_1**, **ITEM=2**,
**MEASURES=20100**, `OBS_VALUE`, percentage of economically active residents
aged 16+. The corresponding **ITEM=1** supplies published model-based counts;
**MEASURES=20701** supplies companion confidence values. No sex/age subgroup
dimension is selected. Counts were inspected diagnostically, not adopted as an
additional predictor or used to calculate the rate.

Numerator: model-based ILO unemployed residents (including the jobless
seeking-work/available-to-start population). Denominator: economically active
people aged 16+, not all residents or the 16–64 population. The publisher's
[2006 model guide](https://www.ons.gov.uk/file?uri=/employmentandlabourmarket/peopleinwork/employmentandemployeetypes/methodologies/subnationallabourmarket/modelbasedestimatesunemploymenttcm77252969.pdf)
describes estimating it using survey employment plus modelled unemployment.
The guide explains random area effects, claimant-count auxiliary information,
model dependence and overlapping annual periods. It is historical methodology
documentation, not proof that every implementation detail stayed unchanged
through 2026. Do not replace the published rate with a Task 010 population ratio.

The rate matches the approved definition; no substitution or clarification of a
different population is needed. Dataset-level metadata says `Persons`, while the
selected rate is percent: the item-specific source labels/official rate table
control its interpretation. Generic measures metadata returns claimant-rate
descriptions for other measure IDs; those are not definitions for this UMB rate.
Retain source metadata without inheriting unrelated denominator definitions.

### Earlier history, uncertainty and revisions

Two advertised official [ONS M01 historical workbooks](https://www.ons.gov.uk/employmentandlabourmarket/peoplenotinwork/unemployment/datasets/modelledunemploymentforlocalandunitaryauthoritiesm01/current)
were retrieved successfully. Their internal publication statements identify
12 April and 16 August 2022, independently of retrieval time. They contain
**1996/97–2003/04** March-to-February estimates, preceding the API's rolling
calendar-based history. In each of those eight early periods, the latest archive
has 318 English LA source rows, 288 exact cohort code rows and 273 numeric
matched rates. Full missing-code lists are in
[archive evidence](../reports/data_audits/009.3/archive-evidence.json). Do not
rename those periods as calendar years or assume a continuous homogeneous
294-authority history beginning in 1996. Earlier fixed-effects research is
described by ONS but its files/cohort coverage were not verified here.

The archived `LA,UA Rates` sheet labels companion columns `+/- (%)` and describes
95% intervals. Hartlepool, source row 8, April 2021–March 2022, has rate
6.77162394 and margin 1.4031907932; these match Nomis's 6.8 and 1.4 at its
published precision. Thus `20701` is retained as the published confidence
margin, not a second rate or CV; the archived evidence supports its percentage-
point/95% interpretation. Current independent Nomis HTML and CSV agree on
Hartlepool July 2025–June 2026: count 2,300, count confidence 600, rate 5.5,
rate confidence 1.6. No new bounds, unemployment ratios or modelling features
were calculated.

Across the primary extract, 48,928 observations have `A` (normal value) and
2,576 have `Q` (missing); all have publication-confidentiality code `F` (free).
Missing values are not zeros, and `OBS_CONF` is a confidentiality flag rather
than the numeric confidence margin. No duplicate observation keys or other
suppression flags were observed in this extract. Missing confidence alone does
not establish why an estimate is unavailable or invalidate the published rate.
Sampling uncertainty, survey non-response/input quality, model assumptions and
shrinkage remain limitations; more precise model estimates do not prove absence
of bias or predictive usefulness. Avoid interpreting adjacent overlapping periods
as independent observations or inventing acceptance/CV/completeness thresholds.

The two M01 workbooks share 28,644 code/period cells; 28,200 are numeric in both,
and **2,876 stored numeric rate cells differ**. This includes all coded source
LA/UA rows and underlying precision; it is not a count of visibly rounded
cohort revisions. The later file notes June 2022 LFS reweighting affecting
2020–2022. Its final-edition note points future LA updates to LI01/Nomis.
Other advertised M01 versions were inventoried, not all downloaded. These
accessible vintages do not establish a complete first-publication archive.

### Publication evidence and limitations

Nomis confirms current release/update metadata **20 October 2026 at 07:00:00**
for July 2025–June 2026. Preserve the source timestamp literally: its timezone
is not certified here, so this is not a verified UTC availability timestamp.
Dataset FirstReleased is 29 March 2006; it cannot date every historic observation.
The empty release-calendar display on the landing page does not mean no releases
occurred. Recent release metadata follows quarterly updates, with varying lags;
no fixed lag or future publication guarantee is approved.

The overview has revision records for 56 of 87 period codes, including 28 with
multiple records. Detailed time metadata has 84 entries, providing additional
historical evidence, but not a certified complete ledger. Population reweighting
comments identify 2015/2016/2017 bases, and 2020/2021 correction entries exist.
Some `current`/`revised` labels are not chronologically consistent: retain the
original records rather than automatically selecting a modelling vintage.
Dataset LastRevised (2021) cannot certify no later revisions, as the 2022
archived reweighting note demonstrates. Scheduled time is distinct from released
time and neither is replaced by retrieval time.

Two recent detailed metadata entries contain literal `apsStartDate`/`apsEndDate`
placeholders; older end dates sometimes use the first of the stated end month.
The published reference-period labels and date codes are clear, but those raw
day-level metadata fields cannot be silently treated as trustworthy interval
endpoints. Preserve and flag them in any future contract. No historical feature
join, availability repair or vintage-selection policy is implemented.

The [ONS Explore Local Statistics indicator](https://www.ons.gov.uk/explore-local-statistics/indicators/modelled-unemployment)
currently displays calendar 2004–2023 and a suspension pending APS reweighting
in 2027. That derivative indicator's coverage/suspension is distinct from the
Nomis UMB API, which actually returned 2026 periods. It does not prove Nomis
has stopped updating, nor guarantee future continuity. No general survey-status
claim is inferred for every historical release from a current webpage label.
